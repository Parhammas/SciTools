Understanding the results

Mean: the average of all measurements.

Median: the middle value when data is placed in order.

Range: the difference between the largest and smallest value.

Standard deviation: describes how spread out measurements are from the mean.

Use population standard deviation when the values represent the entire group being studied. Use sample standard deviation when the data is a sample used to estimate a larger population.

Percent Error

Compare an experimental result with an accepted value.

Formula

Percent Error = |Experimental − Accepted| ÷ |Accepted| × 100%

Percent error shows how far an experimental result is from an accepted or theoretical value.

Percent Difference

Compare two experimental measurements.

Formula

Percent Difference = |Value 1 − Value 2| ÷ Average of the two values × 100%

Percent difference compares two measured values when neither is treated as the accepted answer.

EVALUATION

Error & Uncertainty Guide

Identify what may have affected your results and describe it more precisely.

Random Error

Random error causes measurements to vary unpredictably between repeated trials.

Example: reaction time causes slightly different stopwatch readings each trial.

Possible improvement: repeat more trials and calculate an average.

Systematic Error

Systematic error shifts measurements in the same direction because of a consistent problem.

Example: a balance reads 0.50 g too high every time.

Possible improvement: calibrate equipment or correct the procedure.

Measurement Uncertainty

Every measuring device has a limit to how precisely it can be read.

Example: a ruler marked every millimetre cannot provide infinitely precise measurements.

Procedural Mistake

A mistake is different from unavoidable experimental uncertainty.

Instead of simply writing "human error," identify the specific event and explain how it could affect the result.

Avoid: “Human error affected the experiment.”
Better: “Some solution remained on the inside of the graduated cylinder during transfer, so the actual volume delivered may have been lower than the recorded volume.”
